# Business domain rules

Read before touching prices and currencies, client rows (uniqueness, takeover, merges) or device identity. Moved out of `AGENTS.md`; every rule here is as binding as one there.

- **Every amount inside one business is in the business currency.** Joda `Money.plus` throws `CurrencyMismatchException` on mixed currencies, and every total here is a plain `reduce { acc, price -> acc + price }` (`IssueQuoteImpl`, `Appointment.totalAmount`, `AppointmentRequest.totalAmount`), so a mixed-currency business turns quoting and booking into 500s. Hence: `CreateService`/`UpdateService` re-denominate the client's price into the business currency (`Money.inCurrencyOrNull`, `service/business/domain/impl/.../operation/service/BusinessCurrencyPricing.kt`; an amount with more decimals than the currency allows is `PricePrecisionInvalid`, not rounded), and `UpdateBusiness` rejects a currency change once `ServiceDataSource.hasServices` is true (`CurrencyLocked`; re-sending the stored code is always a no-op). Both sides go through `BusinessDataSource.lockBusiness(id, RowLock)`, which locks the business row and returns its currency: service writes take `RowLock.SHARED` (they don't block each other), a currency change takes `RowLock.EXCLUSIVE`, each as the transaction's first statement (see the lock-first rule in `docs/agents/transactions-and-locking.md`). A stored code `CurrencyValidator` rejects (legacy rows such as `usd`) doesn't lock, so the owner can still fix it, and service writes reject it with `BusinessCurrencyInvalid` instead of a 500. A new write that stores a price on a business entity must take the same lock and never trust the currency the client sends.

- **A client's email is unique per business, across detached and integrated clients alike; its phone is not** (`client_business_id_email_unique`, compared case-insensitively under `utf8mb4_uca1400_ai_ci`, so in-memory dedup lowercases; the phone has no index at all since business `V18` dropped `client_business_id_phone_unique` and `client_phone`). A business only ever creates detached clients (`CreateClient` takes `Client.Detached`, the route ignores `ClientRemote.userId`), and no business write looks a contact up in the user service: that would tell any business which phones have an account and hand it the user's profile. A detached client is only a placeholder for a person until they use the app: a user's first booking (`GetAppointmentBookingContextImpl`) takes over the one detached row holding their email (`ClientDataSource.getClientsByEmails` + `takeOverClient`, keeping its `id` — appointments store it as `client_id` — and `description`) and announces it with `BusinessEvent.ClientLinked` (empty `mergedClientIds`), so appointments records the account on that row's history (`LinkClientUser`). A profile email change that collides with a detached row of a business where the user is already integrated deletes that row and links it into the user's client the same way before the sync (`findClientMergesForUser`, `SyncUserProfileImpl`). **Never match a client by phone.** Only the email is verified (sign-up and `ConfirmEmailChange` prove it with a code), so a takeover or merge by phone hands a stranger's client row, its notes and appointment history to whoever typed that phone into their profile; a phone-only match gets a new row instead. User emails are unique too, so two integrated rows can't collide except transiently while two users swap an email. A write that stores a client email relies on the index alone and maps `.onConstraintFailure { }` to `BUSINESS_CLIENT_EXISTS` (`CreateClientImpl`, `UpdateClientImpl`); a lookup before the write is only for reporting per-row results, as in `ImportClientsImpl`, where one violation would otherwise fail the whole batch without naming the row. Batch imports are capped (`ImportClients.MAX_CLIENTS` = 500) because nothing caps a request body: Ktor decodes the whole ProtoBuf list before any check runs, and one transaction holds the whole batch. `EmailValidator` accepts lowercase addresses only and `PhoneValidator` rejects a space before `+`; both rules apply to single creates and imports alike.

**Device identity spans an account and installation.** Authorization and notifications both identify a device association by `(authId, deviceUuid)`, never by installation UUID alone. Notification creation must preserve the existing token and language on replay and reject a conflicting user. `DeviceDeleted` and `DeviceLanguageUpdated` carry `authId`; legacy payloads default it to `Uuid.NIL` and are rejected for manual reconciliation. Push-token routes take `authId` from `AppPrincipal`. Inactive-device deletion returns both fields through `DELETE RETURNING`, and failed sign-up only compensates the remote user: its device and creation outbox entry roll back together, so it must not emit a device-deletion event.
