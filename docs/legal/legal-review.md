# Legal gap review

Reviewed on 2026-11-09: the BookkMe and self-hosting privacy policies and terms in `docs/legal/`, the server's legal-version publication model, and relevant account and appointment storage. This is a drafting and implementation-gap review, not approval for publication or a worldwide legal opinion. The operator's country, target markets and executed provider contracts have not been established. The follow-up [provider and Crashlytics audit](provider-and-crashlytics-audit.md) checks current public terms and the local Android/iOS source and records the operator's retention decisions.

## Legal reference points

Consent must be specific and distinguishable from a contract or privacy notice; consent and contractual necessity are different grounds. See the [European Commission's legal-bases guidance](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/data-protection/information-business-and-organisations/legal-grounds-processing-data_en) and the [EDPB guidance on contractual necessity](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2026-03/edpb-summary-guidelines2-2019-contractual-necessity-online-services_en.pdf).

The GDPR requires appropriate conditions for sensitive data, transparent notices, request handling, processor arrangements and international transfers. Relevant provisions are Articles 5–9, 12–14, 17–22, 27–28, 32–36, 44–49 and 77. The ordinary rights-response period is one month; permitted extensions require timely explanation. Indirect collection raises separate notice obligations. These references identify review areas; they do not establish that a particular basis or transfer mechanism is available to BookkMe. See the [GDPR official text](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/PDF/?uri=CELEX%3A32016R0679).

Device-storage and access rules are separate from the GDPR basis. Their scope extends beyond cookies, so an opt-out and legitimate-interest wording do not resolve whether default-on diagnostics need prior consent. Exemptions need an assessment of the actual operations and local rules. See [EDPB Guidelines 2/2023, final version](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2024-10/edpb_guidelines_202302_technical_scope_art_53_eprivacydirective_v2_en_0.pdf).

For relevant EU digital-service contracts, conformity remedies and modifications have statutory conditions; withdrawal has its own scope and exceptions. A service being free does not, by itself, answer whether those regimes apply. See [Directive 2019/770, Articles 3 and 19](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/ALL/?uri=CELEX%3A32019L0770) and [Directive 2011/83, Articles 3, 6 and 9–16](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/ALL/?uri=CELEX%3A32011L0083).

Choice of contract law and international jurisdiction are distinct, with protected consumer rules. The new clauses preserve rather than assume away those rules. See [Rome I, Article 6](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A32008R0593) and [Brussels I bis, Articles 17–19](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/ALL/?uri=CELEX%3A32012R1215).

For Poland, the electronic-service terms must address scope, technical requirements, prohibited illegal content, contract formation/termination and complaints, and be available before contracting in a form users can retain. See [the Electronic Services Act, Article 8, consolidated text](https://eli.gov.pl/api/acts/DU/2002/1204/text/U/D20021204Lj.pdf). Where the Consumer Rights Act applies, check Articles 7a, 7aa, 12, 27–38 and 43h–43q: complaint responses generally have a 14-day deadline with consequences for silence, some sole traders receive protection, and adverse digital-service changes can require durable notice and termination rights. Applicability to this free service and each booked service requires assessment. See [the Consumer Rights Act, consolidated text](https://isap.sejm.gov.pl/isap.nsf/download.xsp/WDU20140000827/U/D20140827Lj.pdf). Use provisions effective on the launch date; the consolidated files also mark future amendments.

## Unresolved publication dependencies

| Priority | Item | Concrete next step and evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Before publication | Operator and markets | Supply legal identity, establishment, registration, address and countries served; obtain local review and fill law, regulator, jurisdiction and consumer-information values. A translation cannot make one legal regime universal. |
| Before business onboarding | BookkMe DPA and provider arrangements | The BookkMe-to-business DPA covering clients and employees is drafted in all three languages and kept with the BookkMe texts; publish it at `DPA_URL`, publish a new version before a subprocessor change, and notify owners of a breach without undue delay. The follow-up audit confirms that standard provider terms incorporate their processing arrangements, so separate signatures should not be assumed necessary. Keep applicable contract records, identify any custom terms and obtain required subprocessor authorisations. |
| Before international processing | Transfer inventory | Public transfer arrangements for Cloudflare, Postmark and Firebase have been checked and linked in the BookkMe notice. Only the company's employees and director access production data, from the countries where they are located and never from Ukraine (operator decisions 2026-11-11 and 2026-11-12); that is not a Chapter V transfer, so no access-country list is kept, and a contractor would need a DPA and an Annex 3 entry before getting access. Hosting contracts, any custom agreements and supplementary-measure assessment remain outside the evidence reviewed. The follow-up audit distinguishes vendor DPF provisions from independently verified registry status. |
| Before diagnostics in affected markets | Crashlytics and website technologies | Both apps turned Crashlytics off by default on 2026-11-11; it is opt-in in Settings and the policies give consent as the basis. Google's removal process starts after 90 days; the policy wording is corrected. Device tests and website-cookie inspection remain open. |
| Before sensitive-data use | Health data and professional services | Choose supported use cases, a lawful controller workflow, access restrictions and a deletion process; assess DPIA/DPO and any sector rules. Consider health inferences from service names, clinic identity and appointment details as well as notes. If those controls are absent, keep sensitive data out of the tool. A note or a terms checkbox cannot supply the missing workflow. |
| When handling erasure requests | Retained appointment records | The operator has chosen to retain appointment history while the business exists, with no age-based deletion. Separate client/account erasure still clears contact details and notes, while reasons and identifiers remain. Only employees write cancellation and price-adjustment reasons, and the terms forbid personal details in them; if one still identifies the client, redact it when handling that client's erasure request. |
| Known GDPR gap, accepted by the operator on 2026-11-12 | Support mailbox provider | Mail to the legal and support addresses goes through Cloudflare Email Routing to a consumer Gmail account and is answered through Postmark SMTP. Consumer Gmail offers no Article 28 agreement and no transfer safeguard, so Google holds that mail as its own controller; the BookkMe policy discloses this in sections 6 and 7. Close it by moving the mailbox to a provider with a DPA (Google Workspace, Zoho Mail on its EU data centre after confirming it signs a DPA for the plan, or the hosting provider's mail), then update both policies' provider tables, the record of processing and `bookkme/provider-agreements/`. Until then, do not handle a business's workspace data by email. |
| Before relying on current retention | Support mailbox and required business records | Support emails and their attachments live only in the mailbox; delete them there 24 months after the last message. Automatic client/owner erasure may conflict with a business's external record-keeping duties; do not claim an in-app legal hold exists. |
| Before publishing rights promises | Requests outside normal app actions | Establish an intake log, deadlines, identity alternatives, processor escalation and manual handling of restriction, objections, correction and third-party data. `DataExports` enforces one app export daily and a 72-hour document lifetime; those mechanics cannot be the only statutory request route. |
| Before targeting consumers | Consumer disclosures and notice process | Complete `CONSUMER_INFORMATION`, verified technical requirements and complaint deadlines. Assess the booking-tool contract separately from the underlying appointment service. Implement any required durable confirmations and advance change notices; an app acceptance prompt is insufficient evidence. |
| Before enabling minors' accounts | Capacity and children | Validate the minimum age, guardian-authorisation route where applicable, and handling of appointments concerning children. The existing drafts' minimum age is a product rule, not proof of capacity or parental consent. |
| Before public business listings | Moderation and platform classification | Confirm the support/report channel, ability to act and review mistakes. Assess whether the EU Digital Services Act applies, the relevant service category, representative/contact requirements and any exemptions; this review does not decide that classification. See [the DSA, including Articles 11–17, 19–20 and 30](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A32022R2065). Check app-store reporting/blocking requirements separately. |
| Before publishing operational claims | External settings and restoration | Postmark message retention is now confirmed as 45 days by the operator and public documentation; suppression records can remain longer. Open and link tracking is disabled on every email by the sender. Encryption, backup locations and deletion after restore still need actual operational evidence. Keep historical accepted documents immutable. |
